N° 10

Before you ask

The questionseverybody asks.

Will I be lonely?

Honestly, it depends on the month. We are starting out, so early groups are small — you may be the only guest for a while. There is a welcome dinner, a coworking space full of other remote workers in Pokhara, and a host family in the village. If a busy social scene is what you need, Pokhara in high season suits you better than a village in winter.

What if I get sick or have an accident?

Pokhara has hospitals and clinics used to treating travellers. From a village, we plan the route to the nearest clinic before anyone stays there. Travel insurance that covers trekking is required — we will ask to see it, because a helicopter evacuation from the mountains is not something you want to discover you are paying for.

Can I extend, or leave early?

Extending is usually possible within the 150-day visa limit, and we handle the immigration side. Leaving early is always allowed — it is your life — but the refund depends on what we have already paid out to the room, the homestay and the guide. We will put the exact terms in writing before you pay.

Is it safe, including for women travelling alone?

Nepal is generally safe for solo travellers, and Pokhara particularly so. In a village you are living inside a family home, which is safer than a hotel in most respects and less private in others. We will be straight with you about both, and you can speak to the host family before you commit.

Do I really need to trek?

No. The trek is included, not compulsory. Poon Hill is four to five days over a long weekend and is suitable for most reasonably fit people with no mountaineering experience. If you would rather not, say so and we will arrange something else.

Who is this not for?

Anyone who needs guaranteed uninterrupted power, same-day deliveries, a gym chain, or a nightlife scene. Also anyone whose work cannot survive an occasional outage. We would rather lose the booking than have you arrive disappointed.
